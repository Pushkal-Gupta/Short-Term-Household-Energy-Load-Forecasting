\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Data}
We use the public release of the Low Carbon London smart-meter trial run by UK Power Networks~\cite{ukpn2015lcl}.
It contains half-hourly consumption of 5{,}567 London households between November 2011 and February 2014. We stream
all 168 files of the partitioned release (\RowsInPeriod{} million readings in the study year), observe
\HouseholdsSeen{} household identifiers, and apply the following rules. The study period is calendar year 2013.
Only standard-tariff households are kept, because the time-of-use group received dynamic price signals in 2013 that
would confound the comparison. Unparseable, negative or implausible readings ($>10$\,kWh per half-hour) are set to
missing, duplicate timestamps are averaged, and each series is re-indexed to a full 30-minute grid. Gaps of up to
2\,h are linearly interpolated and longer gaps are left missing; any window that touches a missing value is dropped.
Households with at least 95\,\% coverage in 2013 are eligible (\HouseholdsEligible{} homes). From these we draw a
cohort of \NumHouseholds{} homes, stratified into quintiles of training-period mean load (20 per quintile) with a fixed
selection seed. The cohort's mean training-period load is 0.206\,kWh per half-hour (range 0.008--0.972), and its mean
coverage is 99.9\,\%.

The split is chronological and identical for every household: training January--August 2013, validation
September--October and test November--December. A window belongs to the split of its target time stamp, so its
inputs may reach back into the previous split; those readings are known at forecast time. This yields 1{,}160{,}952
training and 292{,}052 test windows. Scalers, load-profile clusters and the heterogeneity index are computed from
training data only, and every decision (early stopping, best federated round, hyper-parameters) uses validation error
only. Test error never drives a decision.

\subsection{Experimental matrix}
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} lists the experiments. Unless stated otherwise, community mode uses $K=20$ clients of five
homes each, every client participates in every round, $E=1$ local epoch and $T=30$ rounds. Thirty rounds of one
local epoch equal the number of passes over the data allowed to the centralised arm (30 epochs, early stopping with
patience 4), so federated and centralised training have the same computational budget. FedProx's $\mu$ and
HA-FedProx's $\mu_0$ were tuned on validation data from the grids $\{10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\}$ and
$\{10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}$, under the hardest (cluster) partition and with the first seed. This selected $\mu=\TunedMuFedProx{}$
and $\mu_0=\TunedMuHA{}$, which were then used in every other experiment. Each experiment is repeated with three
seeds (42, 43, 44), which change model initialisation, client sampling, stragglers and the random and Dirichlet
groupings, but not the cohort, the profile clusters or the split.

\subsection{Implementation}
The pipeline is written in Python with PyTorch. The federated loop is a deterministic in-process simulator whose
clients implement the Flower \texttt{NumPyClient} interface. An optional backend runs the same clients through
Flower's own FedAvg and FedProx strategies~\cite{beutel2020flower}, and on a test configuration it reproduces the
simulator's per-round validation error and final test error exactly. Experiments ran on a single Apple M5 Max
workstation (Metal backend), eight runs in parallel. Code, configuration files and every result table are available
in the accompanying repository (Section~\ref{sec:data}).
