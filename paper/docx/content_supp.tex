\section{Run-level tests}
Table~\ref{tab:stats} lists every comparison in the paper. Households scored by one trained model are not independent,
so the unit of replication is the training run: each run contributes its pooled test MAE. Where both arms ran with the
same seeds, the differences were paired by seed (paired $t$-test); otherwise Welch's $t$-test was used. With every
household present in every run, this is equivalent to testing the arm in a linear mixed model with crossed random
effects for household and run. The Holm correction was applied over all rows together, and it is the only correction
used in the paper. $\Delta$MAE is A minus B; negative values favour A. The share of households doing better under A is
descriptive only.

\section{Heterogeneity-adaptive proximal coefficient}
The original submission proposed a rule that sets the proximal coefficient from the disagreement between client
updates. After round $r$ the server holds the updates $\Delta_k=w_k-w_r$ and their weighted mean $\bar{\Delta}$, and
computes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
\rho_r=\frac{\sum_k p_k\lVert\Delta_k-\bar{\Delta}\rVert^2}{\sum_k p_k\lVert\Delta_k\rVert^2}\in[0,1],\qquad \mu_{r+1}=\mathrm{clip}\left(\mu_0\rho_r,\ 10^{-4},\ 1\right).
\end{equation}
The rule never improved on FedAvg. Under the cluster partition its run-level difference from FedAvg was
$\Delta=\RvTsHaAvgDiff{}\times10^{-4}$\,kWh (95\,\% CI \RvTsHaAvgLo{} to \RvTsHaAvgHi{}, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}$\,\RvTsHaAvgP{}),
and \RvTsHaAvgHH{}\,\% of households did better under it. Its signal $\rho_r$ ranked partitions by heterogeneity (Spearman
\RhoHSpearman{} with $H_{\mathrm{JS}}$, $p$ \pv{\RhoHSpearmanP}, $n=\RhoHRuns{}$ runs), but on average it rose from
\RhoRoundOne{} in round 1 to \RhoLast{} in the last round, a change about \RhoWithinOverBetween{} times its spread across
partitions. As the shared descent direction shrinks during training, any residual disagreement pushes $\rho_r$ towards
one, so a coefficient proportional to it grows late in training whatever the data. Table~\ref{tab:hetfull} gives all
three aggregation rules for each grouping.
